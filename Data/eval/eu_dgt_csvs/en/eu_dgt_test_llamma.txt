State aid in the sense of Article 61(1) of the EEA Agreement
Article 61, paragraph 1 of the EEA Agreement reads as follows:
The authority for surveillance will assess whether the criteria for the existence of State aid within the meaning of Article 61(1) of the EEA Agreement are met for i) the regulation modified by the supplementary regulation and ii) the five investment agreements.
Classification as state aid requires that a benefit is granted by the state or from state funds.
In the context of the aiding provisions, the term state encompasses both regional and local institutions.
A revenue loss in taxation is a consumption of public funds, as in the case of tax expenditures; state support can be granted not only through tax laws, regulations and administrative provisions, but also through the practice of tax administration.
A reduction of the tax assessment basis or a complete or partial reduction of the tax amount, fees, or charges implies a revenue loss and is therefore equivalent to the use of public funds in the form of tax expenditures.
The regulation amended by the supplementary decree
In its decision No. 390/10/COL, the supervisory authority concluded that the regulation represents a transfer of state funds.
The changes associated with the supplementary regulation have no impact on this conclusion.

Depreciation regulations have been granted various exemptions:

Preferential treatment of certain companies or branches of industry
In the decision No. 390/10/COL, the monitoring authority concluded that the regulation is selective because only companies that invest in certain areas in Iceland, which are eligible under Article 61(3)(c) of the EEA Agreement, have a claim to the corresponding aid.
The supervisory authority accepts the argumentation of the Icelandic authorities that the changes to the regulation resulting from the supplementary regulation increase the benefits granted by the regulation for Becromal.
The amendments introduced by the supplementary regulation thus constitute a selective measure, as they only affect a specific company.
Accordingly, the supervisory authority concludes that the supplementary regulation constitutes a benefit for certain companies or product branches pursuant to Article 61, paragraph 1 of the EEA Agreement.
Even among the beneficiaries of aid in the framework of the five investment agreements, it involves individual companies, the measures are therefore selective.
The term state aid is broader than the term subsidy, as it encompasses not only positive benefits such as subsidies themselves, but also state measures that in various forms reduce the burdens that a company regularly has to bear and which, although they do not represent subsidies in the strict sense of the word, are equivalent to them in terms of kind and effect.
According to constant case law, a measure by which state authorities grant certain companies a tax exemption is considered a state or state-funded subsidy, even if it is not linked to the transfer of state funds, as it puts the beneficiary financially in a better position than other taxpayers.
The five investment agreements enable the beneficiaries to be exempt from part of the costs that they would normally have to bear in the course of their business activities, such as the payment of the applicable corporate tax at a certain point in time, the municipal statutory land value tax as well as the generally applicable social insurance contributions.
The supervisory authority therefore concludes that the five investment agreements favour certain companies and sectors in the sense of Article 61, paragraph 1 of the EEA Agreement.
Competition distortions and impairment of trade between the contracting parties
In accordance with constant case law, it is not necessary to prove an actual impact of the aid on the trade between the member states and an actual distortion of competition for the qualification of a national measure as state aid, but only to examine whether the aid is capable of impairing this trade or distorting competition.
If a benefit granted by an EFTA member state strengthens the position of a company against other competitors in trade within the EEA, it must be considered as being influenced by the benefit.
In its decision No. 390/10/COL, the supervisory authority concluded that the regulation has strengthened the competitive position of the supported companies in the eligible areas compared to actual or potential competitors in the EEA.
The Becromal Investment Agreement strengthens Becromals competitive position against other manufacturers of anodized aluminum foil for electrolytic capacitors.
Becromal has confirmed that the market for anode foils in aluminum electrolytic capacitors is a global market and that Becromals customers include companies from [...] and [...].
The fact that the largest competitors of Becromal are companies from [...] does not mean that the agreement has no impact on trade in the EWR.
The Verne investment agreement strengthens the competitive situation of Verne compared to other data centers in Iceland.
In addition, Verne operates a global data center for the pre-performance market, whose performance is available to customers in the EWR as well as on the world market.
The Kísilfélagið Investment Agreement strengthens the competitive situation of Kísilfélagið compared to other manufacturers of metallurgical silicon and silicon dioxide dust.
The market for metallurgical silicon and silicon dioxide dust is a global market.
The Thorsil Investment Agreement strengthens the competitive situation of Thorsil compared to other manufacturers of silicon metal.
According to the preamble of this investment agreement, the project was initiated, inter alia, to meet the increased demand for silicon metal in Europe and North America.
The GMR-Endurvinnslan Investment Agreement strengthens the competitive situation of GMR Endurvinnslan compared to other recycling companies and steel manufacturers.
The company intends to deliver most of its products to buyers in the construction industry.
Accordingly, the supervisory authority concludes that the five investment agreements strengthen the competitive situation of the respective supported companies in the eligible areas compared to their actual or potential competitors in the EWR.

In its opening decision, the surveillance authority expressed doubts as to whether the aid granted within the framework of the investment agreements was granted in accordance with the regulation approved by the surveillance authority, since the agreements appeared to have been impaired by changes to the regulation made by the supplementary regulation.
Pursuant to Article 1, paragraph c of Protocol 3, Part II, modifications to existing aid are to be considered as new aid.
According to Article 4, paragraph 1 of the Decision No 195/04/COL of the Supervisory Body on the implementing provisions of Article 27 of Part II of Protocol 3 (the Implementing Decision)  [...] any alteration of an existing aid is considered to be a restructuring of the aid, except for a change of a formal or administrative nature which cannot have an influence on the assessment of the compatibility of the aid measure with the Common Market.
Changes that do not affect the fundamental nature of the subsidy therefore do not affect the classification of the measure.
The changes made by the supplementary regulation make it possible to grant aid for projects that were begun even before the aid was granted, yes, even before the regulation came into effect.
The changes made by the supplementary regulation alter the fundamental characteristics of the regulation.
Particularly, the requirement for a stimulus effect was removed from the regulation.
This requirement formed a crucial part of the assessment of compatibility for aid measures within the framework of the originally approved regulation (see description in Decision No 390/10/COL).
In accordance with this, the supervisory authority holds the view that these changes to the regulation, which were made by the supplementary regulation, represent a fundamental change to the originally approved regulation.
Considering the fact that, according to Article 1, paragraph c of Annex II, modifications to existing aid are to be considered as new aid, the question arises whether the modified existing aid is to be classified as a whole or only the modifications as new aid.
In its judgment in the Gibraltar case, the court of first instance decided:
Consequently, the supervisory authority must determine whether the changes made by the supplementary regulation can be distinguished from the original regulation.
If this is the case, then the changes constitute new aid and must therefore be re-evaluated in the context of Article 61, paragraph 3, letter c, and the Guidelines of the Surveillance Authority for State Aid with Regional Focus 2007-2013 (the Regional Aid Guidelines 2007).
In constant case law, an amendment to an existing aid regulation that extends the regulation to a new category of beneficiaries is clearly separable from the original regulation, since the application of the existing aid regulation to the new category of beneficiaries has no impact on the assessment of the compatibility of the original regulation.
The altered wording of Article 3, letter c of the Investment Incentive Ordinance states that an aid can also be granted for a project that began before the entry into force of the supplementary ordinance, but after the conclusion of a specific investment agreement.
This change extended the scope of application of the regulation and went beyond the scope of application established by Decision No 390/10/COL of the Supervisory Authority, as aids can now be granted for projects that were already begun before the regulation came into force and would therefore not have passed the examination with regard to the incentive effect established in the approved regulation.
By the supplementary regulation, a regulation was thus opened up for a new category of beneficiaries.
In addition, the supervisory authority is of the opinion that the changes introduced by the supplementary regulation to Articles 8 and 20 of the investment incentive regulation, i.e. the reduction of the reference interest rate for corporate tax and the extension of the maximum duration of tax exemption, were only introduced in order for this new category of beneficiaries to benefit from this regulation.
These new beneficiaries can thus receive more aid than was the case under the originally approved regulation.
These changes to the regulation are fundamentally connected with one another.
These changes do not affect the compatibility of individual measures that were already introduced within the framework of the originally approved regulation.
The supervisory authority considers these changes resulting from the supplementary regulation as changes that can be clearly separated from the original regulation.
Consequently, the supervisory authority holds the view that these changes to the regulation constitute a new aid measure.
Furthermore, it follows that any aids granted on the basis of the supplementary regulation are also to be considered as new aids.
The supervisory authority is therefore dealing with the assessment of the compatibility of the changes to the regulation by the supplementary ordinance.
In its Decision No 390/10/COL on the approval of the measure, the supervisory authority states the following:

The conditions and methods for testing the presence of an incentive effect in an aid, as described in the regional aid guidelines 2007, formed a prerequisite for the approval of the regulation by the supervisory authority.
The supplementary regulation, on the other hand, added to the wording of Article 3 of the Investment Incentive Regulation the phrase or on the basis of a special investment agreement that was concluded prior to the entry into force of the law and thus also enabled the granting of aid for projects that had already begun before the regulation came into effect.

The Becromal Investment Agreement pertains to an investment in a facility for the anodizing of aluminum foil, which was to be built in two phases in the city of Akureyri.
The first phase should be completed by the end of March 2011, the second phase is to be completed by the end of 2014; investment costs were estimated at a total of around 117.25 million EUR.

In your view, this change, which allowed the company Becromal to apply for investment incentives under this regulation, was necessary for the Icelandic authorities to be able to meet their contractual obligations with Becromal.
Reference to a special [...] investment agreement [...] prior to the law coming into effect was therefore taken over into the investment incentive regulation by the supplementary ordinance, so that Becromal would also fall under this regulation.
Becromal could have had the required examination of the incentive effect solely due to the changes introduced by the supplementary regulation.
The supervisory authority has already explained that the changes introduced by the supplementary regulation constitute a new form of aid that can be distinguished from the original regulation.
This results in the fact that the aids granted within the framework of the Becromal investment agreement are to be considered as new aids, since the agreement was concluded against the backdrop of the investment incentive ordinance amended by the supplementary regulation.

For completeness and to address the points raised by Becromal and the Icelandic authorities, the supervisory authority has alternatively regarded the Becromal investment agreement as an ad-hoc measure.
For this purpose, the supervisory authority refers to the regional aid guidelines 2007 and, in particular, to the condition contained therein regarding the incentive effect.
According to point 30 of the regional aid guidelines 2007, the presence of an incentive effect is a necessary prerequisite for the compatibility of the aid.
The aim of the provisions in point 30 of the regional aid guidelines 2007 is to give the competent authority the opportunity to check whether an investment aid creates a sufficient incentive effect without carrying out a complete analysis of the economic preconditions at the time of the investment decision of the recipient.
If such a check is not ensured before the work on the project is taken up, the entire project cannot receive any subsidies, is therefore not eligible for funding.
As confirmed by the court in the Kronoply case judgment, the supervisory authority may rely on a temporal sequence for its assessment of the incentive effect.
Becromal argued that the legal assessment of aid cases has changed with the introduction of a more refined economic analysis and that the supervisory authority must also evaluate the economic nature of the incentive effect (i.e., whether the aid contributed to a changed behavior of the aid recipient) when assessing it and also take into account the provisions of the LIP guidelines.
The supervisory authority cannot follow this argument.
Becromal refers to the judgment of the Court in the matter of HGA, in order to argue that: … the Court of Justice decided in its examination of the judgment of the first instance court that the only decisive factor for determining whether the aid had an incentive effect was to establish whether it changed the behavior of the investors, and that the first instance court ignored all references to the case of Kronoply.
Becromal, however, disregards the explicit opinion of the court in its interpretation of the judgment in the case HGA, that [...] the requirement that the aid application must precede the start of the provision of aid is a simple, appropriate and reasonable criterion that enables the Commission to presume the necessity of the planned aid was not disputed in the appeal.
The supervisory authority holds the view that in this case, it is appropriate to base the finding of incompatibility on a time sequence, namely that i) the examination of the incentive effect as required by the regional aid guidelines 2007 was not fulfilled and ii) the works on the project were already started before the conclusion of the investment agreement and before the grant of aid.
The supervisory authority also rejects the argument of Becromal that the supervisory authority must investigate the plan in the context of the LIP guidelines and apply the examination of the incentive effect as described there.
The supervisory authority states that for certain large investment projects, the examination of the incentive effect in accordance with point 30 of the regional aid guidelines 2007 (on the basis of the chronological requirement that a grant application must first be submitted and eligibility for funding established before work begins) is supplemented by a second examination of an economic nature (on the basis of an economic analysis in relation to the contribution of the grant to a change in the behavior of the grant recipient) in accordance with point 57 of the regional aid guidelines 2007 and the LIP guidelines.
The LIP guidelines foresee an in-depth examination for certain major investment projects, i.e. those that have significant effects on trade and could lead to substantial distortions of competition.
According to point 3 of the LIP guidelines and point 49 of the regional aid guidelines 2007, a major investment project is considered a first investment with eligible expenses of over 50 million EUR.
As previously mentioned, the Becromal investment agreement covers investment costs of a total of €117.25 million for both phases of the project.
However, neither Becromal nor the Icelandic authorities have provided the surveillance authority with information regarding the intensity of the assistance or the eligible costs for the project.
Furthermore, none of these parties submitted information to the supervisory authority on the conditions of the detailed scrutiny obligation in accordance with the LIP guidelines, to which point 57, letter a and b of the regional aid guidelines 2007 also refer (i.e. that the beneficiary of the aid is responsible for more than 25% of the sales of the relevant product on the relevant market and that the capacity created by the investment project is more than 5% of the market for the relevant product).
The supervisory authority holds the view that nothing indicates that the Becromal project exceeds the limits set in point 57 of the regional aid guidelines 2007.
The supervisory authority concludes that the incoming analysis described in the LIP guidelines is not applicable to the Becromal investment agreement.
Consequently, in accordance with prevailing case law and in accordance with the literal wording of the regional aid guidelines 2007, it is sufficient to determine whether the recipient of aid has submitted an aid application; in addition, the competent authority must confirm the measure in writing before the work begins, but the aid is dependent on approval by the supervisory authority.
According to the Regional Aid Guidelines 2007, the start of the works is to be understood as the commencement of construction work or the first binding commitment to order equipment, excluding feasibility studies.
As confirmed by the Icelandic authorities and Becromal, construction work on the Becromal project began in 2008 and thus before the signing of the earlier agreement on July 7, 2009.
The supervisory authority cannot follow the argumentation presented by Becromal that the start of work should be limited to the ordering of equipment specifically manufactured for the relevant project, and that the acquisition of land and/or the construction of a factory does not necessarily represent an investment decision, as such an investment could be easily disposed of at any time.
This interpretation is supported by the case law of the court, according to which even small investments by the beneficiary of aid can have legal consequences; they thus represent the first phase of work.
In addition, the information provided to the regulatory authority indicates that Becromal began construction of the facility at the beginning of 2008 and that the recruitment procedure was started later in that year.
Similarly, they observed that the additional regulation had no impact on the Verne investment agreement since there was no investment agreement concluded beforehand with Verne.
According to Icelandic authorities, an investment agreement had been initialed (but not signed) on October 23, 2009.
That is why the Icelandic authorities argued that there had been no prior investment agreement with Verne that could constitute aspecial investment agreement under the supplementary regulation.
However, the Authority also noted that it did not, at the time, have sufficient information regarding the status of this project.
In their response, the Icelandic authorities stated that:
The construction works of the Becromal project began following the investment agreement concluded on May 29, 2009, prior to the conclusion of the Becromal investment agreement on December 30, 2010.
The Kísilfélagið project had not yet begun at the time the Icelandic authorities transmitted their observations to the Authority.
The agreement signed on May 29, 2009, pertains to an exemption from exchange restrictions and has no impact on the respect of the incentive effect.
There is no link either between the Verne investment agreement of September 27, 2011 and the draft agreement of October 23, 2009 which had been notified to the Authority before being withdrawn later on.

State aids in the sense of Article 61, paragraph 1, of the EEA Agreement
Article 61, paragraph 1, of the EEA Agreement is worded as follows:
The Authority will examine below whether the criteria for the existence of state aid within the meaning of Article 61, paragraph 1, of the EEA Agreement are met for i) the regime as modified by the supplementary regulation and ii) the five investment agreements.
To be considered state aid, a benefit must be granted by the state or with the aid of state resources.
For the purposes of the application of state aid rules, the term State also covers regional and local entities.
A loss of tax revenue is equivalent to the consumption of state resources in the form of tax expenditures and public aids may be provided both through legislative, regulatory, or administrative tax provisions as well as through the practices of the tax administration.
A reduction in the tax base or a total or partial reduction of the amount of tax, duties or charges, implies a loss of revenue and therefore corresponds to the consumption of state resources in the form of fiscal expenditure.

Measures favoring certain companies or certain productions
The Authority concluded, in Decision No 390/10/COL, that the scheme was selective because only enterprises investing in certain eligible regions of Iceland could benefit from aid under Article 61, paragraph 3, point c), of the EEA Agreement.
The Authority accepts the argument of the Icelandic authorities that the changes made to the regime by the supplementary regulation extend the benefits granted under the regime to Becromal.
These modifications therefore constitute a selective measure, given that they concern a specific enterprise.
The Authority concludes, as a result, that the supplementary regulation favors certain enterprises and certain productions within the meaning of Article 61, paragraph 1, of the EEA Agreement.
Similarly, the beneficiaries of the five investment agreements being determined companies, the measures are selective.
The notion of aid is more general than that of subsidy, in that it includes not only positive benefits, such as subsidies themselves, but also state interventions that, in various forms, lighten the charges normally weighing on a companys budget and, in doing so, without being subsidies in the strict sense of the word, are of the same nature and have the same effects.
According to constant case law, a measure by which public authorities grant certain companies a tax exemption that, although not involving a transfer of state resources, places the beneficiaries in a more favorable financial situation than other taxpayers constitutes a state aid granted by the state or through state resources.
The five investment agreements allowed beneficiaries to be relieved of part of the costs they would normally have had to bear themselves in the context of their activity, such as the applicable corporate tax at a given time, the normal municipal land tax, and the generally applicable social security contribution.
The Authority concludes, therefore, that the five investment agreements favour certain enterprises and certain productions within the meaning of Article 61, paragraph 1, of the EEA Agreement.
Distortion of competition and its impact on trade between contracting parties
It is constant jurisprudence that for the purposes of qualifying a national measure as state aid, it is not a matter of establishing a real incidence of aid on trade between contracting parties and an actual distortion of competition, but only of examining whether the aid is capable of affecting these exchanges and distorting competition.
In addition, when an aid granted by an EEA state strengthens the position of a company in relation to other competing companies in intra-EEA trade, these latter must be considered as influenced by the aid.
The Authority had concluded, in decision no 390/10/COL, that the regime strengthened the competitive situation of the beneficiary undertakings in the eligible regions compared to that of their real or potential competitors within the EEA.
The investment agreement concluded with Becromal strengthens the competitive position of this company in relation to that of other producers of anodized aluminum foil for electrolytic capacitors.
As confirmed by Becromal, the market for anode foil used in aluminum electrolytic capacitors is a global market, and among Becromals clientele are companies [...], and [...].
The fact that Becromals main competitors are companies [...] does not mean that there is no effect on trade within the EEA.
The investment agreement concluded with Verne strengthens the competitive position of this company compared to other data centers established in Iceland.
In addition, Verne operates a global large data center whose services will be made available to customers within the EEA and on the global market.
The investment agreement concluded with Kísilfélagið strengthens the competitive position of this company compared to that of other metallurgical silicon and silica dust producers.
The global market for metallurgical silicon and silica dust is worldwide.
The investment agreement concluded with Thorsil strengthens the companys competitive position compared to other silicon metal producers.

This company plans to export the majority of its products to sell to buyers in the construction sector.

According to article 1, point c), of part II of protocol 3, any modification of an existing aid must be considered as a new aid.
In accordance with Article 4, paragraph 1, of Decision No 195/04/COL of the Authority concerning the implementing measures referred to in Article 27 of Part II of Protocol 3 (the implementing decision), [...] bymodification of an existing aid is meant any change other than purely formal or administrative changes that are not capable of affecting the assessment of the compatibility of the aid measure with the common market.

The amendments made by the supplementary regulation provide for aid to be granted for projects whose work began before the aid was granted – in fact, even before the regime came into effect.
The changes made by the supplementary regulation modify the fundamental characteristics of the regime.
In particular, the requirement for a motivating effect provided for by the regime has been abolished.
This requirement was an essential element in the assessment of the compatibility of aid measures granted under the regime as initially authorized (decision no 390/10/COL).

The Authority also rejects Becromals argument that the Authority must examine the project in light of the guidelines on major investment projects and apply the incentive effect criterion prescribed therein.
The Authority notes that for certain major investment projects, the incentive effect criterion provided for in point 30 of the 2007 guidelines (based on the chronological requirement that an application has been submitted and eligibility confirmed before the start of works) is complemented by a second criterion of an economic nature (based on an economic analysis that the aid contributes to a change in the behavior of its beneficiary), provided for in point 57 of the guidelines on regional aid and in the guidelines on major investment projects.
Guidelines on major investment projects provide for a thorough assessment for certain major investment projects, namely those that have significant effects on trade and may lead to notable distortions of competition.
According to point 3 of the guidelines on major investment projects and point 49 of the 2007 guidelines, a major investment project is an initial investment whose eligible expenses exceed 5,000,000 EUR.

The Organs will subsequently evaluate whether the criteria for state aid in the sense of Article 61, paragraph 1, of the EEA Agreement are met for i) the regime as amended by the Supplementary Regulation, and ii) the five investment agreements.
For it to be considered state aid, an advantage must be granted by the State or through state funds.
For the purposes of state aid rules, the term State also covers regional and local entities.
A loss of fiscal income is equivalent to the consumption of state funds in the form of fiscal expenses, and state aid can also be granted through legislative, regulatory or administrative fiscal provisions, as well as through fiscal administration practices.
A reduction in the taxable base or a total or partial reduction in the amount of taxes, rates or charges implies a loss of tax revenue and therefore corresponds to the consumption of state resources in the form of fiscal expenditure.

The Body concludes therefore that the supplementary Regulation favours certain companies and certain productions in the sense of Article 61, paragraph 1, of the EEA Agreement.
In the same way, the beneficiaries of the five investment agreements are individual companies, and therefore the measures are selective.
The definition of aid is more general than that of subsidy, as it encompasses not only the positive benefits, such as subsidies proper, but also state interventions that, in various forms, alleviate the burdens that normally fall on a companys budget, and therefore, not being subsidies in the strict sense of the word, have a similar character and produce the same effect.
According to reiterated jurisprudence, a measure by which public authorities grant tax exemptions to certain companies, although it does not involve a transfer of state funds, places those subject to the exemption in a more favorable financial situation than the rest of taxpayers, constitutes a state aid or through state resources.
The five investment agreements allowed beneficiaries to be freed from part of the costs they would normally have had to bear in their business activity, such as paying corporate tax at the applicable rate at a given time, municipal property tax and general social security contributions.
The Body concludes, therefore, that the five investment agreements favour certain companies and certain productions in the sense of Article 61, paragraph 1, of the EEA Agreement.
Distortion of competition and its impact on trade between the contracting parties
According to repeated case law, for the purpose of classifying a national measure as state aid, it is not necessary to prove the actual incidence of the aid on trade exchanges between contracting parties and the effective distortion of competition, but rather to examine whether such aid can affect said exchanges and distort competition.
In addition, when an aid granted by an EEA State serves to strengthen the position of a company vis-à-vis other companies competing with this one in intra-EEA exchanges, such exchanges must be considered affected by the aid.
The Body came to the conclusion in Decision 390/10/COL that the regime strengthened the competitive position of beneficiary undertakings in eligible regions, in comparison with their real or potential competitors in the EEA.
The Becromal investment agreement strengthens Becromals competitive position compared to other producers of anodized aluminum foil for electrolytic condensers.
As Becromal has confirmed, the anodized foil market used in aluminum electrolytic capacitors is global, and among Becromals clients are companies in [... ] and [...].
So, the fact that Becromals main competitors are companies of [...] does not mean there are no effects on trade in the EEE.
The Verne investment agreement strengthens Vernes competitive position compared to other data centers in Iceland.
Furthermore, Verne manages a global wholesale trade data center, whose services will be available to EEA and global market clients.
The Kísilfélagið investment agreement strengthens Kísilfélagiðs competitive position relative to other metalurgical silicon and silica powder producers.
The global silicon metal and silica powder market.
The Thorsil investment agreement strengthens Thorsils competitive position compared to other metal silicon producers.
As stated in the preamble of the Thorsil investment agreement, the project was initiated, among other things, to meet a rise in demand for metallic silicon in Europe and North America.
The GMR Endurvinnslan investment agreement strengthens GMR Endurvinnslans competitive position compared to other steel recycling companies and steel producers.
The company intends to export the majority of its products to buyers in the construction sector.
In consequence, the Body concludes that the five investment agreements strengthen the competitive situation of the beneficiary companies in the eligible regions compared to their real or potential competitors in the EEA.

In accordance with Article 1, letter c), of Part II of Protocol 3, modifications to existing aid shall be considered as new aid.
In accordance with Article 4, paragraph 1, of Decision No 195/04/COL, relating to the implementing provisions of Article 27 of Part II of Protocol 3 (hereinafter referred to as the Decision on Implementation), [...] by modification of an existing aid, any change that does not constitute a change of a purely formal or administrative nature without repercussions for the evaluation of the compatibility of the aid measure with the common market shall be understood.
Therefore, modifications that do not affect the essence of the aid do not affect how the measure should be classified.
The modifications introduced by the supplementary Regulation allow for grants to be awarded to projects where work has begun before the grant was awarded, even before the regime came into effect.
The modifications introduced by the supplementary Regulation alter the basic characteristics of the regime.
In particular, the incentive regime requirement has been abolished.

In accordance with reiterated jurisprudence, a modification to an existing aid regime that extends it to a new category of beneficiaries can be clearly distinguished from the initial regime, since the application of the existing aid regime to the new category of beneficiaries does not affect the evaluation of the compatibility of the initial regime.
The revised drafting of Article 3, subsection c), of the Regulation on incentives stipulates that aid may also be granted to a project that began after the signing of a special investment agreement and before the entry into force of the supplementary Regulation.

In the interest of thoroughness and in order to respond to the allegations made by Becromal and the Icelandic authorities, the Body has examined the Becromal investment agreement as an ad hoc measure.
In this regard, the Body refers to the DAR of 2007 and, in particular, to the incentive requirement that appears in them.
In accordance with section 30 of the 2007 DAR, the existence of an incentive effect is a necessary condition for compatibility.
The objective of section 30 of the DAR of 2007 is to allow the Body to determine if investment aid has a sufficient incentive effect, without carrying out a complete analysis of the economic circumstances of the investment decision of the beneficiary at the time it was made.

Therefore, as the General Court confirms in the Kronoply judgment, the Authority may base its assessment of the incentive effect on a chronological circumstance.
Becromal has argued that the assessment of state aid matters has changed with the introduction of a more detailed economic analysis, so that when evaluating the incentivizing effect of the aid, the Body must also evaluate its economic nature (i.e. whether it has contributed to a change in the behavior of the aid recipient), taking into account the provisions of the Guidelines on regional aid for large investment projects.
The Organ cannot accept this argument.
Becromal refers to the judgment of the Court of Justice in the case HGA to argue that: [...] the Court, in its examination of the judgment of the First Instance Court, establishes that the only factor that determines whether the aid measure had an incentivizing effect is to examine whether it has changed the investors behavior, and did not take into account all the references made by the First Instance Court to the Kronoply case.
However, Becromals interpretation of the HGA judgment fails to take into account the explicit statement of the Court of Justice that the General Courts appreciation that the anteriority of the request for aid with respect to the start of the application of the investment project is a simple, relevant and appropriate criterion which allows the Commission to presume the necessary character of the planned aid was not put into question in the appeal procedure.
Therefore, the Organ considers that in this case, it is necessary to base its conclusion of incompatibility on a chronological evaluation: namely, that i) the incentive requirement established in the 2007 DAR was not met, and ii) work on the project began before the celebration of the investment agreement and the granting of aid.
The Body also rejects Becromals claim that the Body must examine the project in light of the Guidelines on regional aid for large investment projects, and apply the incentive effect requirement established in them.
The Body notes that, for large investment projects, the incentive effect test established in point 30 of the 2007 DAR (based on the chronological requirement that an application has been submitted and eligibility confirmed before work begins) is complemented by a second test of an economic nature (based on an economic analysis that the aid contributes to the change in behavior of the beneficiary of the same), provided for in point 57 of the Guidelines on Regional Aid and in the Guidelines on Regional Aid for Large Investment Projects.
Guidelines on regional aid for large investment projects foresee an in-depth evaluation of certain large investment projects, that is, those with significant effects on trade and which may give rise to substantial distortions of competition.
According to paragraph 3 of the Guidelines on regional assistance for large-scale investment projects and paragraph 49 of the 2007 DAR, a large-scale investment project is understood to be an initial investment whose eligible expenses exceed 50 million EUR.
As mentioned earlier, the Becromal investment agreement refers to 117.25 million EUR as the total cost of investment for the two phases of the project.
However, neither Becromal nor the Icelandic authorities have presented any information to the Body regarding the intensity of the aid or the subsidizable expenses of the project.
They have not provided information on the conditions of the detailed verification obligation established in the Guidelines for Regional Assistance to Large Investment Projects referred to in paragraph 57, letters a) and b), of the 2007 DAR (i.e., that the beneficiary of the aid has more than 25% of the products in question in the reference market, and that the production capacity created by the project represents more than 5% of the reference product market).
In the opinion of the Body, nothing indicates that the Becromal project exceeds the maximum limits foreseen in paragraph 57 of the 2007 Guidelines.
The Body concludes, therefore, that the in-depth analysis foreseen in the Guidelines on regional aid for large investment projects does not apply to the Becromal investment agreement.
Consequently, in light of the jurisprudence and the drafting of the DARs of 2007, it suffices to verify if the request for aid has been submitted by the beneficiary and if the authority granting the aid has delivered a written confirmation, conditional on the approval of the measure by the Organ, before the works on the project begin.
The 2007 DAR defines the term commencement of works as the start of the construction work, or the first firm commitment for the order of equipment, excluding prior viability studies.
As confirmed by the Icelandic authorities and Becromal, construction work began on the Becromal project in 2008, even before the signing of the previous agreement of July 7, 2009.
The Organ cannot accept Becromals thesis that thestart of work should be limited to orders of equipment designed specifically for the project in question, and that the acquisition of land or the construction of a plant does not necessarily signal the beginning of an investment decision, since such an investment could be easily sold at a given time.
This interpretation is corroborated by the case law of the Supreme Court, according to which even a reduced level of investment by the beneficiary is sufficient to produce legal effects, and it is therefore considered to be the first phase of the works.
On the other hand, the information at the disposal of the Organ shows that Becromal began the construction of the plant at the beginning of 2008, and its contracting procedure started at the end of that year.
