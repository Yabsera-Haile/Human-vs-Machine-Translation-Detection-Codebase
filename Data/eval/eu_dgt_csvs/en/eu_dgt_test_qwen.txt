State subsidies in accordance with Article 61 Paragraph 1 of the EWR Agreement
Article 61 Paragraph 1 of the EWR Agreement reads as follows:
Below the following, the supervisory authority will evaluate whether the conditions for the existence of state aid in accordance with Article 61 Paragraph 1 of the EWR Agreement are met for i) the amended regulation due to the supplementary ordinance and ii) the five investment agreements.
The classification as a state subsidy assumes that a benefit is provided by the state or from state funds.
In the sense of the aid provisions, the term State covers both regional and local institutions.
A revenue loss from taxation is a consumption of state resources as in the case of tax expenditures; state support can not only be granted through tax laws, ordinances, and administration regulations, but also by practice of tax administration.
A reduction of the tax base or a complete or partial exemption of the tax amount, fees, or charges implies a loss of income and thus equates to the use of state funds in the form of tax expenditures.

Preference for certain companies or production sectors
In the Decision No. 390/10/COL, the supervisory authority concluded that the regulation was selective, as only companies investing in certain areas of Iceland that are deemed eligible for support under Article 61(3)c of the EWR Agreement have a claim to corresponding aid.
The oversight authority accepts the argument of Icelandic authorities that the amendments accompanying the supplementary ordinance will increase the benefits granted by the regulation for Becromal.
The changes adopted by the supplementary ordinance therefore constitute a selective measure as they only affect a specific company.
Consequently, the supervisory authority concludes that the supplementary ordinance constitutes a favouring of certain enterprises or product sectors according to Article 61 Paragraph 1 of the EWR Agreement.
Even among the beneficiaries of the five Investment Agreements, it concerns individual companies, hence the measures are selective.
The concept of state aid is broader than the concept of subsidy, as it encompasses not only positive actions such as subsidies themselves but also state measures that reduce regular burdens faced by a company, which although not strictly subsidies, are equivalent in nature and effect.
A measure granting a state agency to certain companies a tax exemption, which does not involve the transfer of state funds but makes the beneficiaries financially better off than other taxpayers, constitutes a state aid or aid provided with state funds.
The five investment agreements enable the beneficiaries to free themselves from a part of the costs they would normally have to bear as part of their business activities, such as the payment of applicable corporate tax at a certain point in time, the statutory municipal tax, and the general social insurance contributions.
Therefore, the supervisory authority concludes that the five investment agreements favor certain companies and production sectors in line with Article 61 Paragraph 1 of the EWR-agreement.
Competition Manipulations and Disadvantageous Impact on Trade Between the Contracting Parties
Qualification of a national measure as state aid does not require proof of actual effects on trade between Member States and distortion of competition, but only examination whether the aid is appropriate to impair trade or distort competition.
If a beeholding granted by an EFTA member state strengthens a companys position vis-à-vis other competitors in trade within the EWR, this company must be seen as influenced by the beeholding.
In its Decision No. 390/10/COL, the monitoring authority concluded that the regulation has strengthened the competitive position of the supported companies in the eligible areas vis-à-vis actual or potential competitors in the EWR.
The Becromal Investment Agreement strengthens Becromals competitive position compared to other producers of oxidized aluminum foil for electrolytic capacitors.
Becromal has confirmed that the market for anode foils in aluminum electrolytic capacitors is a global market and that customers of Becromal include companies from [...] and [...].
The fact that the largest competitors of Becromal are companies from [...] does not mean that the agreement has no impact on trade in EWR.
The Verne Investment Agreement strengthens Vernes competitive position vis-à-vis other computing centers in Iceland.
Furthermore, Verne operates a global computing center for the preposition market, whose capacity is available both to customers in EWR and on the world market.
The Silicon Feangaði Investment Agreement strengthens Silicon Feangaðis competitive position compared to other producers of metallurgical silicon and silicon dioxide dust.
The market for metallurgical silicon and silicon dioxide dust is a global market.
The Thorsil Investment Agreement strengthens Thorsils competitive position compared to other silicon metal producers.
According to the preamble of this investment agreement, the project was brought forward to satisfy the increased demand for silicon metal in Europe and North America.
The GMR-Endurvinnslan Investment Agreement strengthens GMR Endurvinnslans competitive position compared to other recycling companies and steel manufacturers.
The company intends to deliver most of its products to suppliers in the construction industry.

In her opening decision, the supervisory authority expressed doubts about whether the aid granted within the framework of investment agreements was in accordance with the regulation approved by the supervisory authority, as the agreements appeared to have been adversely affected by changes to the regulation that were implemented through the supplementary ordinance.
According to Article 1, Paragraph c of the Protocol No. 3, Part II, modifications of existing aids are to be considered as new aids.
According to Article 4 Paragraph 1 of Decision No. 195/04/COL of the Monitoring Authority on the Implementing Regulations for Part II Article 27 of Protocol No. 3 (the Implementing Decision), … a restructuring of an existing assistance is regarded as such any change, other than a formal or administrative technical change, that cannot influence the assessment of the compatibility of the assistance measure with the Common Market.
Changes that do not affect the fundamental nature of the assistance do not influence the classification of the measure.
The changes made by the supplementary ordinance allow grants for projects that have already begun before the grant is awarded, and even before the regulation comes into force.
The changes made by the supplementary ordinance alter the fundamental characteristics of the regulation.
Especially the requirement of an incentive effect from regulation was removed.
This assumption formed a crucial part of the compatibility assessment for grant measures within the originally approved regulation (see description in Decision No. 390/10/COL).
Consequently, the supervisory authority maintains the view that the amendments made to the regulation by the supplementary ordinance represent a fundamental change in the originally approved regulation.
Given that according to Article 1, Paragraph c, Part II of Protocol 3 changes to existing aids should be considered as new aids, the question arises whether the changed existing aid should be assessed in its entirety or only the changes as new aids.
In its judgment in the Gibraltar case, the first-instance court decided:
Therefore, the supervisory authority must be worthy of assessing whether the amendments made by the supplementary ordinance can be separated from the original regulation.
Is this the case, then the changes constitute a new subsidy and must therefore be re-evaluated in the context of Article 61 Paragraph 3 Letter c and the Guidelines for State Subsidies with Regional Objectives 2007-2013 (the Regionalsubsidyguidelines 2007) by the supervisory authority.
A change to an existing assistance regulation that expands the regulation to a new category of beneficiaries is clearly separable from the original regulation, as applying the existing assistance regulation to the new category of beneficiaries does not have any impact on the assessment of the original regulations compatibility.
The amended wording of Article 3, Paragraph c of the Investment Incentives Regulation states that a subsidy can also be granted for a project that started after the conclusion of a specific investment agreement but before the entry into force of the supplementary regulation.
This change expanded the scope of the regulation and went beyond the scope established by the Supervisory Authority in Decision No. 390/10/COL, as aid would henceforth also be granted for projects that had already begun before the regulation came into force and whose examination regarding the approved regulations incentive effect would therefore not have been successful.
The supplementary regulation thus opened up the regulation for a new category of beneficiaries.
Moreover, the supervisory authority believes that the amendments introduced by the supplementary ordinance to Articles 8 and 20 of the Investment Incentives Ordinance, i.e., the reduction of the corporate tax rate reference interest rate and the extension of the maximum period for tax exemption, were introduced solely so that this new category of beneficiaries can benefit from this regulation.
These new beneficiaries can thus receive more subsidies than was the case under the originally approved regulation.
For this reason, the changes in the regulation are fundamentally interlinked.
These changes however do not impair the compatibility of individual measures that have already been introduced within the scope of the originally approved regulation.
The supervisory authority considers the amendments accompanying the supplementary ordinance as changes that can be clearly distinguished from the original regulation.
Therefore, the supervisory authority views these changes to the regulation as a new assistance measure.
Moreover, this implies that any subsidies granted on the basis of the supplementary ordinance should also be regarded as new subsidies.

For completeness and to address the arguments put forward by Becromal and the Icelandic authorities, the supervisory authority has appreciated the Becromal Investment Agreement as an ad hoc measure.
For this purpose, the supervisory authority refers to the Regional Assistance Guidelines 2007 and in particular to the incentive effect requirement contained therein.
According to Regulation 30 of the Regional Assistance Guidelines 2007, the presence of an incentive effect is a necessary precondition for the compatibility of the assistance:
The provisions in paragraph 30 of the Regional Assistance Guidelines 2007 aim to give the supervisory authority the possibility to verify whether an investment grant creates sufficient incentive effects without undertaking a complete analysis of the economic prerequisites at the time of the recipients investment decision.
Should such a verification not be ensured before the works on the project commence, the entire project cannot receive subsidies, and therefore is not eligible for support.
As confirmed by the court in its ruling in the case Kronoply, the supervisory authority can base its assessment of the incentive effect on a temporal scope as its foundation.
Becromal claimed that the legal assessment of assistance measures had changed with the introduction of a more refined economic analysis, and thus the supervisory authority should also evaluate the incentive effect and consider the economic nature of the aid (i.e., whether the aid contributed to a change in the behavior of the beneficiary of the aid), taking into account the LIP guidelines.
The oversight agency cannot follow this argument.
Becromal refers to the courts judgment in the case HGA, in order to argue that: …the court decided during its review of the first-instance judgment that the only decisive factor for determining whether the accessory had an incentive effect was whether it had changed the behavior of the investors, and all references by the first-instance court to the legal issue Kronoply were disregarded.
Becromal disregards the explicit opinion of the court in its interpretation of the HGA judgment, which stated that …that the requirement that the benefit claim precede the start of the benefit payment is a simple, substantiated and appropriate criterion that allows the Commission to infer the necessity of the planned benefit was not questioned under the legal means.
Therefore, the supervisory authority holds the view that it is appropriate to base the determination of incompatibility on a temporal framework, namely that i) the examination of the incentive effect as prescribed by the Regional Assistance Guidelines 2007 was not fulfilled and ii) the work on the project had already been undertaken prior to the conclusion of the investment agreement and the provision of the grant.
The oversight authority also rejects Becromads argument that the oversight authority should examine the project in light of the LIP guidelines and apply there described examination of incentive effects.
The oversight authority states that for certain large investment projects, the examination of the incentive effect according to Article 30 of the Regional Assistance Guidelines 2007 (based on a chronological requirement that an assistance application be submitted first and the eligibility for assistance determined before work begins) is supplemented by a second economic assessment (based on an economic analysis regarding the impact of the assistance on the beneficiarys behavior) according to Article 57 of the Regional Assistance Guidelines 2007 and the LIP guidelines.
The LIP guidelines require a thorough review for certain large investment projects, that is, those projects that have significant effects on trade and could lead to substantial distortions of competition.
According to Point 3 of the LIP guidelines and Point 49 of the Regionalbeihilfeleitlinien 2007, a large investment project is an initial investment with eligible expenditures over 50 million EUR.
As already mentioned precedingly, the Becromal Investment Agreement covers investment costs amounting to a total of 117.25 million EUR for both phases of the project.
However, neither Becromal nor the Icelandic authorities have transmitted information regarding the intensity of the assistance or the cost-effectiveness for the project.
Furthermore, none of these parties has submitted information to the supervisory authority regarding the conditions of the detailed examination obligation according to the LIP guidelines, which also refers to points 57 letter a and b of the regional assistance guidelines 2007 (i.e., that the beneficiary is responsible for more than 25% of the sales of the relevant product on the relevant market, and that the capacity created by the investment project exceeds 5% of the market for the relevant product).
The oversight authority maintains the view that there is nothing indicating that the Becromal project exceeds the limits set by paragraph 57 of the Regional Assistance Guidelines for 2007.
The oversight authority concludes that the detailed analysis described in the LIP guidelines is not applicable to the Becromal investment agreement.
Therefore, according to current jurisprudence and as stated in the guidelines for regional assistance 2007, it is sufficient to establish whether the beneficiary of the aid has submitted an aid application; additionally, the competent authority must formally confirm the measure before works commence, but the aid is subject to approval by the supervisory authority.
According to the Regional Assistance Guidelines 2007, beginning of works is understood as the intake of construction works or the first binding commitment to order equipment, with feasibility studies being excluded.
As confirmed by Icelandic authorities and Becromal, construction works on the Becromal project began in 2008, thus before the earlier agreement signed on July 7, 2009.
The oversight authority cannot follow Becromals argument that the start of works should be limited to equipment ordered specifically for the relevant project, and that the acquisition of land and/or construction of a factory building does not necessarily constitute an investment decision, as such an investment could easily be sold again at any time.
This interpretation is supported by the courts precedents, according to which even small contributions from the beneficiary can have legal effect; they thus constitute the first phase of the work.
Furthermore, the information available to the supervisory authority indicates that Becromal started construction of the facility at the beginning of 2008 and that the installation procedure was launched during the latter part of this year.

State aids as defined in Article 61, paragraph 1 of the EEE Agreement
Article 61, paragraph 1, of the EEE agreement is stated as follows:
The Authority will examine whether the criteria for the existence of state aids within the meaning of Article 61, paragraph 1, of the EEE Agreement are met for i) the regime as modified by the supplementary regulation and ii) the five investment agreements.
To be qualified as state aid, an advantage must be granted by the State or through state resources.
For the application of rules regarding state aids, the term State also covers regional and local entities.
A revenue shortfall is equivalent to the consumption of state resources in the form of fiscal expenditures, and public assistance can be provided through tax provisions legislative, regulatory, or administrative measures, as well as through fiscal administration practices.
A reduction of the taxable base or a reduction, total or partial, of the amount of tax, duties, or charges implies a loss of revenue and therefore amounts to the consumption of state resources in the form of fiscal expenditures.

The Authority had concluded in Decision No. 390/10/COL that the regime constituted a state resource transfer.
The modifications made by the supplementary regulation do not alter this conclusion.

The leasing of lands or immovable properties at a price lower than market value results in revenue losses and thus constitutes a transfer of state resources.
Given what has been said before, the Authority concludes that state resources are also involved in the five investment agreements.
Measures favoring certain enterprises or certain productions
The Authority concluded in Decision No. 390/10/COL that the regime was selective as only enterprises investing in certain regions of Iceland eligible for aid under Article 61, paragraph 3, point c), of the EEA Agreement could benefit from such aid.
The Authority accepts the Icelandic authorities argument that the amendments made by the supplementary regulation extend the benefits granted under the regime to Becromal.
These modifications therefore constitute a selective measure, given that they concern a determined enterprise.
The Authority concludes, accordingly, that the supplementary regulation favors certain enterprises and certain productions in the sense of Article 61, paragraph 1, of the EEE Agreement.
Similarly, beneficiaries of the five investment agreements being determined enterprises, the measures are selective.
The notion of aid is more general than that of subsidy, given that it encompasses not only positive payments such as subsidies themselves, but also state interventions that, in various forms, alleviate the burdens normally weighing on a companys budget and, by doing so, though not strictly speaking subsidies, are of the same nature and have identical effects.
According to a consistent jurisprudence, a measure by which public authorities grant tax exemptions to certain enterprises, although not involving a transfer of state resources, places beneficiaries in a more advantageous financial position than other taxpayers constitutes an aid granted by the state or through state resources.
The five investment agreements have allowed beneficiaries to be relieved of a part of the costs they would normally have to bear in the course of their activities, such as corporate income tax at a given time, normal municipal property tax, and general social security contributions.
Therefore, the Authority concludes that the five investment agreements favor certain enterprises and certain productions as defined in Article 61, paragraph 1, of the EEA Agreement.
Market distortion and effect on exchanges between the contracting parties
It is constant in jurisprudence that for the qualification of a national measure as state aid, it is not necessary to establish a real impact of the aid on the trade between the contracting parties and an effective distortion of competition, but only to examine if the aid is capable of affecting these exchanges and distorting competition.
Furthermore, when assistance provided by an AELE state strengthens an enterprises position relative to other competing firms in intra-EEE exchanges, these latter firms should be considered influenced by the aid.
The Authority had concluded in Decision No. 390/10/COL that the regime strengthened the competitive position of beneficiary enterprises in eligible regions compared to that of their real or potential competitors within the EEA.
The investment agreement concluded with Becromal strengthens the competitive position of this company in relation to other producers of anodized aluminum foil for electrolytic capacitors.
As Becromal has confirmed, the market for anode sheet materials used in electrolytic aluminum capacitors is a global market, and among Becromals clientele are companies such as [...] and [...].
Also that Becromals main competitors are companies [...] does not mean there is no effect on exchanges within the EEE.
The investment agreement concluded with Verne strengthens this companys competitive position compared to other established data centers in Iceland.
Moreover, Verne exploits a global large-scale data center whose services will be made available to clients within the EEE and on the global market.
The investment agreement concluded with Kísilfélagið strengthens this companys competitive position compared to other producers of metallurgical silicon and silica dust.
The market for metallurgical silicon and silica dust is global.
The investment agreement concluded with Thorsil strengthens this companys competitive position compared to other silicon-metal producers.
As indicated in the preamble of said investment agreement, the project was launched primarily to respond to the increase in demand for silicon-metal in Europe and North America.
The investment agreement concluded with GMR Endurvinnslan strengthens this companys competitive position compared to other steel recycling firms and steel producers.
This enterprise plans to export the majority of its products to buyers in the construction sector.
As a result, the Authority concludes that the five investment agreements strengthen the competitive position of beneficiary enterprises in eligible regions compared to that of their real or potential competitors within the EEA.
Given the above, the Authority concludes that the modifications made by the supplementary regulation and the five investment agreements constitute state aids within the meaning of Article 61, paragraph 1, of the EEE Agreement.
In its decision to open the procedure, the Authority expressed doubts as to whether the aid granted within the framework of investment agreements fell within the scope of application of the regime, since the agreements seemed, at first glance, to have been affected by the amendments made to the regime by the supplementary regulation.
According to Article 1, paragraph c) of Part II of Protocol 3, any modification of an existing aid should be considered as a new aid.
By the terms of Article 4, paragraph 1, of Decision No. 195/04/COL of the Authority concerning the execution measures referred to in Article 27 of Part II of Protocol 3 (the Execution Decision), … a modification of an existing aid is understood as any change other than purely formal or administrative modifications that are not of a nature to influence the evaluation of the compatibility of the aid measure with the internal market.
Therefore, changes that do not affect the substance of the aid do not affect how the measure should be classified.
The amendments provided by the supplementary regulation allow for aid to be granted for projects whose works have already started before the award of aid – in fact, even before the entry into force of the regime.
The modifications made by the supplementary regulation modify the fundamental characteristics of the regime.
In particular, the requirement for an incentive effect envisaged by the regime has been removed.
And this requirement constituted an essential element of the assessment of the compatibility of the measures of assistance granted under the regime as initially authorized (decision no 390/10/COL).
Therefore, the Authority considers that the modifications made to the regime by the supplementary regulation constitute a substantial modification of the regime as authorized.
Given that according to Article 1, paragraph c), of Part II of Protocol 3, any modification of an existing aid must be considered as a new aid, the question arises as to whether the modified existing aid should be qualified as a new aid, or whether such qualification is only applicable to the modifications themselves.
In the judgment he rendered in the Gibraltar case, the First Instance Court declared as follows:
Therefore, the Authority must examine whether the modifications made by the supplementary regulation are detachable from the initial regime.
If this is the case, the modifications constitute in themselves a new aid and, accordingly, must be assessed in light of Article 61, paragraph 3, point c), and the guidelines of the Authority concerning regional state aids for the period 2007-2013 (the 2007 Guidelines).
It is constant in jurisprudence that modifying an existing aid regime providing for the extension of it to a new category of beneficiaries is a modification clearly separable from the initial regime, since applying the existing aid regime to the new category of beneficiaries does not affect the assessment of the compatibility of the initial regime.
The new wording of Article 3, paragraph c), of the Regulation on incentive measures states that aid may also be provided to a project that started after the conclusion of a special investment agreement and before the entry into force of the supplementary regulation.
This modification has expanded the scope of application of this regime beyond what was authorized by Decision No. 390/10/COL of the Authority, to the extent that it allowed grants to projects whose works had commenced before the entry into force of the regime and which, as a result, would not have satisfied the incentive effect criterion set out in the regime as authorized.
The supplementary regulation therefore opened the regime to a new category of beneficiaries.
Moreover, the Authority considers that the amendments made by the supplementary regulation to Articles 8 and 20 of the incentive measures regulation, namely the reduction of the corporate income tax reference rate and the extension of the maximum duration of tax exemptions, have been added solely for the benefit of this new category of beneficiaries.
These new beneficiaries can therefore benefit from more significant aids than what was allowed under the regime as authorized.
Also the changes made to the regime are fundamentally linked.
However, they do not have an impact on the compatibility of the different measures already covered by the system as authorized.
The Authority therefore considers that the modifications made by the supplementary regulation constitute a modification clearly detachable from the initial regime.
Therefore, she considers these modifications to constitute new assistance.
Furthermore, any assistance provided on the basis of the supplementary regulation will also be considered as new aid.

For completeness and to respond to the arguments advanced by Becromal and the Icelandic authorities, the Authority appreciated, as a supplementary measure, the investment agreement concluded with Becromal as an ad hoc measure.
For this reason, the Authority refers to the 2007 guidelines and, in particular, to the requirement for an incentive effect that is included there.
According to paragraph 30 of the 2007 guidelines, the existence of an incentive effect is a necessary precondition for compatibility:
The paragraph 30 of the 2007 guidelines aims to allow the Authority to verify if investment aid has sufficient incentive effect without fully examining all economic aspects of the investment decision made by the beneficiary at the time it was granted.
If these guarantees are not provided and the works commence, the entire project loses its right to aid, that is, the project itself is no longer eligible.
Thus, as confirmed by the Court in the Kronoply judgment, the Authority can base its assessment of the incentive effect on a chronological circumstance.
Becromal has noted that the assessment of state aid files has evolved with the introduction of a more refined economic analysis, so that when assessing the incentive effect of aid, the Authority must also evaluate its economic nature (that is, verifying whether it has contributed to a change in the beneficiarys behavior), taking into account the provisions of the guidelines on major investment projects.
The Authority cannot accept this argument.
Becromal invokes the judgment of the Court of Justice in the HGA case to argue that: « […] the Court, upon reviewing the first-instance tribunals decision anew, determined that the sole factor determining whether the aid measure had an incitative effect was to examine whether it had altered the investors behavior and rejected all references that the first-instance tribunal had made to the Kronoply case. »
However, Becromals interpretation of the HGA judgment does not take into account that the Court of Justice expressly noted that the Tribunals conclusion that the priority of the aid request with respect to the start of the investment project execution constitutes a simple, relevant and appropriate criterion enabling the Commission to presume the necessity of the proposed aid has not been challenged in the context of the appeal.
Therefore, the Authority considers it appropriate to base its finding of incompatibility on a chronological assessment: specifically, i) the incentive effect criterion stated in the 2007 guidelines was not met and ii) work had already begun on the project before the investment agreement and aid were concluded.
The Authority also rejects Becromals argument that the Authority should examine the project in light of guidelines for major investment projects and apply the incentive effect criterion prescribed therein.
The Authority observes that for certain large investment projects, the criterion of the envisaged incentive effect mentioned in paragraph 30 of the 2007 Guidelines (based on the chronological requirement that a request had been submitted and eligibility confirmed before the start of works) is supplemented by a second criterion of an economic nature (based on an economic analysis showing that the aid contributes to a change in the beneficiarys behavior). This second criterion is also found in the Guidelines concerning regional aids and in the Guidelines on large investment projects.
Guidelines for large investment projects provide a thorough appreciation for certain large investment projects, namely those that have substantial effects on trade and can lead to significant distortions in competition.
According to Directive 3 on Large Investment Projects and Directive 49 of 2007, a large investment project is an initial investment with eligible expenditures exceeding 50000000 EUR.
As previously indicated, the investment agreement concluded with Becromal mentions a total investment cost of 117250000 EUR for the two phases of the project.
However, neither Becromal nor Icelandic authorities provided information to the Authority regarding the intensity of the aid or the allowable costs related to the project.
Similarly, they have provided no information on the conditions of the detailed verification obligation mentioned in the guidelines for large investment projects, to which points 57(a) and (b) of the 2007 guidelines refer (namely that the beneficiary of the aid realizes more than 25% of the sales of the considered products on the relevant market and that the capacity created by the project represents more than 5% of the market for the considered product).
According to the Authority, nothing indicates that the Becromal project exceeds the thresholds set in paragraph 57 of the 2007 guidelines.
Therefore, the Authority concludes that the detailed analysis foreseen in the guidelines on large investment projects does not apply to the investment agreement concluded with Becromal.
Therefore, taking into account the jurisprudence and the wording of the guidelines from 2007, it is sufficient to verify whether a request for aid was submitted by the beneficiary and whether a written confirmation from the authority responsible for granting the aid was given subject to the authorization of the measure by the Authority before the start of the project works.
According to the 2007 guidelines, the expression début des travaux means either the start of construction work, or the first firm commitment to order equipment, excluding preliminary feasibility studies.
As the Icelandic authorities and Becromal have confirmed, construction work on the Becromal project had begun in 2008 before even the earlier agreement of July 7, 2009 had been signed.
The Authority cannot accept Becromals arguments that the start of works should be limited to the ordering of equipment specially designed for the project in question and acquiring land and/or constructing an installation would not necessarily mark the beginning of an investment decision, as such investments could easily be sold at any time.
This interpretation is supported by the jurisprudence of the Court, according to which even a modest level of beneficiary investment is sufficient to produce legal effects and is therefore considered as constituting the first step in the work.
Moreover, the Authoritys information shows that Becromal began constructing its installation at the beginning of 2008 and that its recruitment procedure started later in the same year.

The Marmeti investment agreement referred to the construction and operation of a fish processing plant in Sandgerði.
The operations of Marmeti included the salting and drying of marine products.
In order to apply the clauses regarding state aid as specified in Articles 61 to 63 of the EEE Agreement, state aid must be granted to companies related to the manufacture of products covered by the EEE Agreement.
Article 8, paragraph 3, of the EEE Agreement establishes the following:
the products included in Chapters 25 to 97 of the Harmonized System of classification and coding of goods, except those enumerated in Protocol 2;
the products included in Protocol 3 and subject to the specific arrangements contained in that Protocol.
Article 8, paragraph 3, of the EEE Agreement therefore limits the subject matter scope of application of the EEE Agreement to the products mentioned above, except in cases where the Agreement provides otherwise.
Fish and aquatic products, except those falling within Chapters 25-97 of the Harmonized System of commodity classification or specified in Protocol 3, are outside the scope of application of the EEA Agreement.
Although Article 4 of Protocol 9 of the EEA Agreement establishes that State aid granted to the fishing sector shall be eliminated when it distorts competition, the competence to evaluate state aid to the fishing sector lies with the Contracting Parties to the EEA Agreement.
In the opinion of the Organ, the products produced by Marmeti fell within Chapters 03, 05, 15, 16 and 23 of the Harmonized System of Classification and Coding of Goods (HS) and thus remained outside the products covered by the EEA Agreement.
Therefore, the Organ does not have competence to evaluate the possible assistance granted in accordance with the Investment Agreement Marmeti.
Based on the above, the Body concludes its investigation into the Marmeti investment agreement.

Next, the Organ will evaluate whether the criteria for the existence of state aid as referred to in Article 61, paragraph 1, of the EEE Agreement are met for i) the regime in its modified version by the Supplementary Regulation, and ii) the five investment agreements.
For something to qualify as state aid, a benefit must be granted by the state or through state funds.
For the application of rules on state aids, the term State also covers regional and local entities.
A loss of tax revenue equals the consumption of state funds in the form of fiscal expenditures, and it can also be provided with state aid through legislative, regulatory, or administrative fiscal provisions, as well as through fiscal administration practices.
A reduction in the taxable base or a partial or total reduction in the amount of taxes, fees, or charges implies a loss of tax revenues and therefore represents a consumption of state resources through fiscal expenditures.

Favoring certain companies or productions
In Decision No. 390/10/COL, the Body concluded that the regime was selective, in the sense that only companies investing in certain regions of Iceland and which can benefit from aid under Article 61, paragraph 3, letter c), of the EEA Agreement, could receive aid under the same.
The Body accepts the arguments of the Icelandic authorities that the resulting regime modifications under the Supplementary Regulation expanded the benefits conferred upon Becromal under the regime.
Therefore, these modifications constitute a selective measure as they affect a specific company.
The Organ concludes therefore that the Supplementary Regulation favors certain companies and certain productions in the sense of Article 61, paragraph 1, of the EEA Agreement.
Similarly, the beneficiaries of the five investment agreements are individual companies, and thus the measures are selective.
The definition of aid is more general than that of subsidy, as it encompasses not only positive payments, such as proper subsidies, but also state interventions that alleviate burdens normally borne by a companys budget, and for this reason, although they are not subsidies in the strict sense of the word, they have a similar character and produce the same effect.
According to repeated precedent, a measure whereby public authorities grant certain companies a tax exemption, although it does not involve a transfer of state funds, places those who benefit from the exemption in a more favorable financial position than the rest of taxpayers, constitutes an aid granted by the State or through state resources.
The five investment agreements allowed beneficiaries to be relieved of part of the costs they would normally have had to bear in their business activity, such as paying the applicable corporate tax, municipal property tax, and general social security contributions.
Therefore, the Body concludes that the five investment agreements favor certain companies and certain productions in the sense of Article 61, paragraph 1, of the EEA Agreement.
Antitrust Manipulation and Its Effect on Trade Between Contracting Parties
According to repeated jurisprudence, for the purpose of qualifying a national measure as state aid, it is not necessary to prove the actual impact of the aid on trade exchanges between the Contracting Parties and effective distortion of competition, but only to examine whether such aid can affect these exchanges and distort competition.
Furthermore, when assistance provided by a Member State of the EELC strengthens the position of a company against other companies that compete with it in intra-EEE exchanges, such exchanges should be considered affected by the assistance.
The Organized reached the conclusion in Decision 390/10/COL that the aid regime strengthened the competitive position of beneficiary enterprises in eligible regions, compared to their real or potential competitors in the EEE.
The Becromal Investment Agreement strengthens Becromals competitive position compared to other producers of anodized aluminum foil for electrolytic capacitors.
As has been confirmed by Becromal, the market for used anodized foil used in aluminum electrolytic capacitors is global, and among Becromals clients are companies in [...] and [...].
Therefore, the fact that Becromals main competitors are companies of [...] does not mean there are no trade effects within the EEE.
The Verne Investment Agreement strengthens Vernes competitive position compared to other data centers in Iceland.
Moreover, Verne manages a wholesale data center of global character, whose services will be available for customers in the EEE and the world market.
The Kísilfélagið Investment Agreement strengthens Kísilfélagiðs competitive position compared to other metallurgical silicon and silica powder producers.
The market for silicon metal and silica powder is global.
The Thorsil Investment Agreement strengthens Thorsils competitive position compared to other metallic silicon producers.
As stated in the preamble of the Thorsil investment agreement, the project was initiated, among other things, to address an increase in demand for metallic silicon in Europe and North America.
The GMR Endurvinnslan Investment Agreement strengthens GMR Endurvinnslans competitive position compared to other steel recycling companies and steel producers.
The company intends to export the majority of its products to construction buyers.
Consequently, the Body concludes that the five investment agreements strengthen the competitive position of the beneficiary enterprises in the eligible regions compared to their real or potential competitors in the EEE.

In its Decision on admissibility, the Body expressed doubts about whether the aid granted within the framework of investment agreements had been provided within the scope of the regime, as the agreements seemed, at first glance, to have been affected by the modifications of the regime resulting from the Supplementary Regulation.
In accordance with Article 1, letter c), of Part II of Protocol 3, modifications to existing aids should be considered as new aid.
By modification of an existing aid, it will be understood any change that does not constitute a purely formal or administrative modification without repercussions for the assessment of the compatibility of the aid measure with the Common Market.
Therefore, modifications that do not affect the essence of the aid do not affect how the measure should be classified.
The modifications introduced by the Supplementary Regulation allow aid to be granted to projects that have already started before the aid is granted, even before the regime enters into force.
The modifications introduced by the Supplementary Regulation alter the basic characteristics of the regime.
In particular, the incentive effect regime requirement has been eliminated.
This requirement was an important part of the compatibility assessment of the assistance measures granted under the regime, as it was initially approved (as described in Decision No. 390/10/COL).
Therefore, the Body considers that the modifications introduced by the Supplementary Regulation constitute a substantial modification of the Regime as it was approved.
Considering that, pursuant to Article 1, letter c), of Part II of Protocol 3, existing aid modifications should be considered new aid, the question arises whether existing modified aid should be considered new aid, or only the modifications themselves.
In its judgment in the Gibraltar case, the First Instance Court declared that:
Therefore, the Body should evaluate whether the modifications introduced by the Supplementary Regulation are separable from the initial regime.
If this were the case, the modification would constitute, as such, a new aid and, therefore, should be evaluated in light of Article 61, paragraph 3, letter c), and the Guidelines of the Committee on State Aid for Regional Objectives for the period 2007-2013 (hereinafter, the 2007 Regional Aid Guidelines).
According to established precedent, modifying an existing aid regime to extend it to a new category of beneficiaries can be clearly dissociated from the initial regime, as the application of the existing aid regime to the new category of beneficiaries does not affect the assessment of the compatibility of the initial regime.
The modified wording of Article 3, letter c), of the Incentives Regulation specifies that aid may be granted also to a project that has started after a special investment agreement has been signed and before the entry into force of the Supplementary Regulation.
This modification extended the scope of the regime beyond what was approved by Decision of the Body No. 390/10/COL, allowing aid to be granted to projects where work had already begun before the regime came into force, and thus would not meet the incentive effect criterion as it was approved.
Therefore, the Supplementary Regulation opened the regime to a new category of beneficiaries.
On the other hand, the Body considers that the modifications introduced by the Supplementary Regulation to Articles 8 and 20 of the Incentives Regulation, that is, the reduction of the reference rate for the corporate tax and the extension of the maximum duration of the tax exemptions, were introduced solely to benefit this new category of beneficiaries.
These new beneficiaries can therefore receive more aid than initially authorized by the initially approved regime.
Therefore, these modifications to the regime are fundamentally interrelated.
Nonetheless, these modifications do not affect the compatibility of the various measures already covered by the approved regime.
Therefore, the Organ considers that the modifications introduced by the Supplementary Regulation constitute a change that can be clearly dissociated from the initial regime.
Therefore, the Body considers that these modifications to the regime constitute a new aid measure.
Moreover, this implies that aids granted on the basis of the Supplementary Regulation will also be considered new aid.
Therefore, the Body proceeds to assess the compatibility of the modifications introduced in the regime by the Supplementary Regulation.

Becromal ha alegado que la evaluación de los asuntos de ayudas estatales ha cambiado con la introducción de un análisis económico más detallado, de manera que a la hora de evaluar el efecto incentivador de la ayuda, el Órgano debe evaluar también su naturaleza económica (es decir, si ha contribuido a un cambio en el comportamiento del beneficiario de la ayuda), teniendo en cuenta asimismo las disposiciones de las Directrices sobre ayuda regional para grandes proyectos de inversión.
The Body cannot accept this argument.
Becromal refers to the judgment of the Court of Justice in the HGA case to argue that: «[…] the Court, in its examination of the judgment of the First Instance Court, establishes that the only factor that determines whether the aid measure had an incentive effect is to examine if it has changed the investors behavior, and did not take into account all the references that the First Instance Court had made to the Kronoply case.»
Nonetheless, Becromals interpretation of the HGA judgment does not take into account the explicit statement by the Court of Justice that the General Courts appreciation that … the prior submission of the aid request with respect to the start of the application of the investment project is a simple, relevant and appropriate criterion that allows the Commission to presume the necessary nature of the planned aid was not challenged in the appeal procedure.
Therefore, the Body concludes that in this case its finding of incompatibility must be based on a chronological evaluation: namely, that i) the incentive requirement established in the DARs of 2007 was not met, and ii) work began on the project before the signing of the investment agreement and the award of aid.
The Organ also rejects Becromals allegation that the Organ should examine the project in light of the Guidelines on regional aid for large investment projects and apply the incentive-promoting requirement established in them.
The Body notes that for some large investment projects, the incentive effect test established in Article 30 of the DARS 2007 (based on the chronological requirement that a request was submitted and eligibility was confirmed before work begins) is complemented by a second economic test of nature (based on an analysis of whether the aid contributes to the beneficiarys behavior change), as specified in the Guidelines on Regional Aid and the Guidelines on Regional Aid for Large Investment Projects.
The Guidelines on Regional Assistance for Large Investment Projects provide a deep evaluation of certain large investment projects, that is, those that have significant effects on trade and may give rise to substantial anti-competitive distortions.
According to Paragraph 3 of the Guidelines on Regional Assistance for Large Investment Projects and Paragraph 49 of the 2007 DAR, a large investment project is understood as an initial investment whose eligible expenses exceed 50 million EUR.
As previously mentioned, the Becromal investment agreement refers to a total investment cost of 117,25 million EUR for the two phases of the project.
However, neither Becromal nor the Icelandic authorities have presented any information to the Organ about the intensity of the aid or the eligible costs of the project.
They have also not provided information about the conditions of the detailed verification obligation established in the Guidelines on Regional Assistance for Large Investment Projects referred to in points 57a and 57b of the 2007 DAR (that is, that the beneficiary of the aid has more than 25% of the products concerned on the reference market, and that the production capacity created by the project represents more than 5% of the reference product market).
In the opinion of the Organ, nothing indicates that Becromals project exceeds the maximum limits set in Paragraph 57 of the 2007 Guidelines.
Therefore, the Organ concludes that the in-depth analysis foreseen in the Guidelines on regional aid for large investment projects does not apply to the Becromal investment agreement.
Therefore, taking into account the jurisprudence and the wording of the DARS of 2007, it suffices to verify if the aid request was submitted by the beneficiary and if the authority granting the aid has provided a written confirmation conditioned upon approval by the Body before the start of work on the project.
The 2007 DAR defines the term start of works as the start of construction works, or the first firm commitment for the equipment order, excluding preliminary viability studies.
As confirmed by Icelandic authorities and Becromal, construction works began on the Becromal project in 2008, even before the signing of the previous agreement on July 7, 2009.
The Body cannot accept Becromals thesis that the start of works should be limited to orders for equipment specially designed for the project in question, and that the acquisition of land or construction of a plant does not necessarily mark the beginning of an investment decision, as such an investment could be easily sold at some point.
This interpretation is corroborated by the jurisprudence of the General Court, according to which even a reduced level of beneficiary investment is sufficient to produce legal effects, and therefore it is considered as the first phase of the works.
On the other hand, the information available to the Organizational Body shows that Becromal began constructing the plant at the beginning of 2008, and its procurement procedure started towards the end of that year.
