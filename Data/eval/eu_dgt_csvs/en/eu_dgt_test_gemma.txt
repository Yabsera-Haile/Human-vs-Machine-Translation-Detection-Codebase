State aid within the meaning of Article 61(1) of the EEC Treaty
Article 61, paragraph 1 of the EEA Agreement reads as follows:
The following will assess the supervisory authority whether the criteria for the existence of state aid within the meaning of Article 61(1) of the EEA Agreement are met for i) the regulation amended by the supplementary regulation and ii) the five investment agreements.
Classification as state aid presupposes that an advantage is granted by the state or from state resources.
In the sense of the provisions on assistance, the term State covers both regional and local institutions.
A tax revenue loss is a consumption of state funds as in the case of tax expenditures; state support can be granted not only through tax laws, regulations and administrative regulations, but also through the practice of tax administration.
A reduction of the tax base or a complete or partial reduction of the tax amount, fees or levies implies a loss of revenue and therefore corresponds to the use of state funds in the form of tax expenditures.
The regulation amended by the supplementary regulation
In its decision No. 390/10/COL, the supervisory authority concluded that the regulation represents a transfer of public funds.
The changes accompanying the supplementary regulation have no impact on this conclusion.

Favoritism towards certain companies or production sectors
In decision No. 390/10/COL, the supervisory authority concluded that the regulation was selective because only companies investing in certain areas in Iceland, which are eligible for aid under Article 61(3)(c) of the EEA Agreement, are entitled to corresponding aid.
The supervisory authority accepts the argumentation of the Icelandic authorities that the changes to the regulation accompanying the supplementary regulation increase the benefits granted to Becromal by the regulation.
The changes incorporated by the supplementary regulation therefore constitute a selective measure, as they only affect a specific company.
Accordingly, the supervisory authority concludes that the supplementary regulation constitutes a favoritism of certain companies or product sectors in accordance with Article 61(1) of the EEA Agreement.
Also among the recipients of aid in the context of the five investment agreements, these are individual companies, so the measures are selective.
The term state aid is broader than the term subsidy, as it not only encompasses positive measures such as subsidies themselves, but also state measures that in various forms reduce the burdens that a company regularly has to bear and which, while not subsidies in the strict sense of the word, are equal to them in nature and effect.
According to consistent case law, a measure by which state authorities grant certain companies a tax exemption, which is not associated with the transfer of state funds, but which financially improves the recipients of aid compared to the other taxpayers, constitutes state aid or aid granted from state funds.
The five investment agreements allow the beneficiaries to be relieved of a portion of the costs that they would normally have to bear themselves in the course of their business activities, such as the payment of applicable corporate income tax at a specific time, the statutory land tax of the municipality, and the generally applicable social security contributions.
The supervisory authority therefore concludes that the five investment agreements favor certain companies and production sectors within the meaning of Article 61(1) of the EEA Agreement.
Market distortions and impairment of trade between the contracting parties
According to consistent case law, the qualification of a national measure as state aid does not require proof of the actual effect of the aid on trade between Member States and actual distortion of competition, but only the examination of whether the aid is capable of affecting such trade or distorting competition.
If aid granted by an EFTA member state strengthens the position of a company vis-à-vis other competitors in trade within the EWR, this must be considered as being influenced by the aid.
In its decision No. 390/10/COL, the supervisory authority concluded that the regulation strengthened the competitive position of the supported companies in the eligible areas compared to actual or potential competitors in the EWR.
The Becromal investment agreement strengthens Becromals competitive position against other producers of anodized aluminum foil for electrolytic capacitors.
Becromal has confirmed that the market for anode foils in aluminum electrolytic capacitors is a global market and that Becromals customers include companies from [...] and [...] .
The fact that Becromals biggest competitors are companies from [...] does not mean that the agreement has no impact on trade within the EEA.
The Verne investment agreement strengthens Vernes competitive position compared to other data centers in Iceland.
Furthermore, Verne operates a global data center for the pre-trade market, whose performance is available to customers both in the EEA and in the global market.
The Kísilfélagið investment agreement strengthens the competitive position of Kísilfélagið against other manufacturers of metallurgical silicon and silicon dioxide dust.
The market for metallurgical silicon and for silicon dioxide dust is a global market.
The Thorsil investment agreement strengthens Thorsils competitive position against other manufacturers of silicon metal.
According to the preamble of this investment agreement, the project was initiated, among other things, to meet the increased demand for silicon metal in Europe and North America.
The GMR Endurvinnslan investment agreement strengthens the competitive position of GMR Endurvinnslan compared to other recycling companies and steel manufacturers.
The company intends to deliver the majority of its products to buyers in the construction industry.
Accordingly, the supervisory authority concludes that the five investment agreements strengthen the competitive situation of the companies supported in the eligible areas vis-à-vis their actual or potential competitors in the EEA.

In its opening decision, the supervisory authority expressed doubts as to whether the subsidies granted under the investment agreements were in line with the regulation approved by the supervisory authority, as the agreements appeared to have been affected by changes to the regulation made by the supplementary ordinance.
According to Article 1, letter c, Part II of Protocol 3, changes to existing aid are to be considered as new aid.
According to Article 4, Paragraph 1 of Decision No. 195/04/COL of the monitoring authority on the implementing provisions of Part II, Article 27 of Protocol 3 (the Implementing Decision) [...] any change, except for a change of purely formal or administrative nature, which has no impact on the assessment of the compatibility of the aid measure with the common market, is deemed to be a restructuring of an existing aid.
Changes that do not affect the fundamental nature of the assistance do not therefore affect the classification of the measure.
The changes made by the supplementary regulation allow for the granting of subsidies for projects that were already started before the grant of the subsidy, and even before the entry into force of the regulation.
The changes made by the supplementary regulation alter the fundamental characteristics of the regulation.
The requirement of an incentive effect from the regulation was particularly removed.
This requirement constituted a crucial element of the compatibility assessment for aid measures under the originally approved regulation (see description in Decision No. 390/10/COL).
Accordingly, the supervisory authority is of the opinion that these changes to the regulation, as made by the supplementary regulation, constitute a fundamental change to the originally approved regulation.
In view of the fact that, according to Article 1, letter c, Part II of Protocol 3, changes to existing aid are to be considered as new aid, the question arises as to whether the amended existing aid should be classified as a whole or only the changes as new aid.

If this is the case, then the changes constitute a new aid and must therefore be reassessed against the background of Article 61(3)(c) and the guidelines of the monitoring authority for state aid with regional objectives 2007-2013 (the Regional Aid Guidelines 2007).
According to consistent case law, a change to an existing aid regulation, by which the regulation is extended to a new category of beneficiaries, is clearly separable from the original regulation, since the application of the existing aid regulation to the new category of beneficiaries has no impact on the assessment of the compatibility of the original regulation.
The amended wording of Article 3, letter c of the Investment Incentive Ordinance states that aid can also be granted for a project that was already started before the entry into force of the supplementary ordinance, but after the conclusion of a special investment agreement.
This amendment expanded the scope of the regulation and went beyond the scope defined in Decision No. 390/10/COL of the supervisory authority, as subsidies can now also be granted for projects that began before the entry into force of the regulation, and the examination regarding the incentive effect defined in the approved regulation would therefore not have been successful.
Through the supplementary regulation, the regulation was thus opened up to a new category of beneficiaries.
Furthermore, the supervisory authority is of the opinion that the amendments introduced to Article 8 and 20 of the Investment Incentive Ordinance by the supplementary regulation, i.e. the reduction of the reference interest rate for corporate tax and the extension of the maximum duration of the tax exemption, were introduced solely so that this new category of beneficiaries could benefit from this regulation.
These new beneficiaries can therefore receive more assistance than was the case under the originally approved regulation.
For this reason, the changes to the regulation are fundamentally interconnected.
These changes do not affect the compatibility of individual measures that have already been introduced within the framework of the originally approved regulation.
The supervisory authority therefore considers these changes accompanying the supplementary regulation as changes that can be clearly separated from the original regulation.
Consequently, the supervisory authority holds the view that these amendments to the regulation constitute a new aid measure.
Furthermore, it follows that any aid granted on the basis of the supplementary regulation is also to be considered as new aid.

For the sake of completeness and to address the arguments put forward by Becromal and the Icelandic authorities, the supervisory authority has alternatively assessed the Becromal investment agreement as an ad-hoc measure.
For this purpose, the supervisory authority refers to the Regional Aid Guidelines 2007 and in particular to the requirement regarding the incentive effect.
According to point 30 of the Regional Aid Guidelines 2007, the existence of an incentive effect is an indispensable prerequisite for the compatibility of the aid.
The aim of the provisions in point 30 of the Regional Aid Guidelines 2007 is to enable the supervisory authority to check whether an investment aid creates sufficient incentive without carrying out a full analysis of the economic conditions at the time of the recipients investment decision.
If such a review is not ensured before the project work begins, the entire project can receive no subsidies, and is therefore not eligible for funding.
As confirmed by the court in the judgment in the case of Kronoply, the supervisory authority can rely on a time frame as a basis for its assessment of the incentive effect.
Becromal argued that the legal assessment of state aid cases had changed with the introduction of a more refined economic analysis, and that the monitoring authority must therefore also assess the economic nature of the aid (i.e., whether the aid contributed to a change in the behavior of the beneficiary) when evaluating the incentive effect, and must also observe the provisions of the LIP guidelines.
The supervisory authority cannot follow this argument.
Becromal refers to the Court of Justices judgment in the HGA case to argue that: […] the Court, in its examination of the judgment of the court of first instance, decided that the only decisive factor in determining whether the aid had an incentive effect was whether it had changed the behavior of investors, and disregarded all references of the court of first instance to the Kronoply case.
Becromal, however, disregards the express opinion of the Court of Justice in his interpretation of the judgment in the case of HGA, […] that the requirement that the application for aid must precede the commencement of the implementation of the aid, is a simple, appropriate and reasonable criterion which enables the Commission to suspect the necessity of the planned aid was not challenged within the framework of the legal remedy.
The supervisory authority therefore holds the view that it is appropriate in this case to base the finding of incompatibility on a time-based process, namely that i) the incentive effect test prescribed in the Regional Aid Guidelines 2007 was not fulfilled and ii) work on the project was already started before the investment agreement was concluded and before the grant was awarded.
The supervisory authority also rejects Becromals argument that the supervisory authority must examine the project against the background of the LIP guidelines and apply the described incentive effect test therein.
The supervisory authority states that for certain large investment projects, the examination of the incentive effect in accordance with point 30 of the Regional Aid Guidelines 2007 (on the basis of the chronological requirement that a grant application must first be submitted and the eligibility for aid determined before work begins) is to be supplemented by a second economic examination (on the basis of an economic analysis of the contribution of the aid to a change in the behaviour of the aid recipient) in accordance with point 57 of the Regional Aid Guidelines 2007 and the LIP Guidelines.
The LIP guidelines provide for a thorough review of certain large investment projects, i.e., those projects that have a clear impact on trade and could lead to significant distortions of competition.
According to point 3 of the LIP guidelines and point 49 of the Regional Aid Guidelines 2007, a large investment project is considered a first-time investment with eligible expenses exceeding 50 million EUR.
As previously mentioned, the Becromal investment agreement covers investment costs of a total of 117.25 million EUR for both phases of the project.
However, neither Becromal nor the Icelandic authorities have provided the supervisory authority with information regarding the intensity of the subsidy or the eligible costs for the project.
Furthermore, none of the parties involved have submitted information to the supervisory authority regarding the conditions of the detailed examination obligation in accordance with the LIP guidelines, to which point 57 letters a and b of the Regional Aid Guidelines 2007 also refer (i.e., that the aid recipient is responsible for more than 25% of the sales of the product in question on the relevant market and that the capacity created by the investment project represents more than 5% of the market for the product in question).
The supervisory authority is of the opinion that nothing indicates that the Becromal project exceeds the limit values set out in point 57 of the Regional Aid Guidelines 2007.
The supervisory authority therefore concludes that the in-depth analysis described in the LIP guidelines is not applicable to the Becromal investment agreement.
Consequently, according to current case law and the wording of the Regional Aid Guidelines 2007, it is sufficient to determine whether the aid recipient has submitted an aid application; furthermore, the competent authority must confirm the measure in writing before the start of the work, but the aid is subject to approval by the supervisory authority.
According to the Regional Aid Guidelines 2007, commencement of work is understood to mean the commencement of construction work or the first binding commitment to order equipment, excluding feasibility studies.
As confirmed by the Icelandic authorities and Becromal, construction work on the Becromal project began in 2008 and thus before the signing of the previous agreement on July 7, 2009.
The supervisory authority cannot follow Becromals argument that the start of work should be limited to the order of equipment specifically manufactured for the project in question, and that the acquisition of land and/or the construction of a factory building does not necessarily constitute an investment decision, since such an investment could be easily resold at any time.
This interpretation is supported by the case law of the court, according to which even small investments by the beneficiary of aid are sufficient to have a legal effect; they thus constitute the first phase of the work.
Furthermore, the information available to the supervisory authority indicates that Becromal began construction of the facility in early 2008 and that the recruitment process was launched later in the year.

The Authority will examine whether the criteria for the existence of State aid within the meaning of Article 61, paragraph 1, of the EEA Agreement are met for i) the regime as amended by the implementing regulation and ii) the five investment agreements.
For a measure to be considered state aid, it must be granted by the State or using State resources.
For the purposes of applying the rules on state aid, the term State also covers regional and local entities.
A loss of tax revenue is equivalent to the consumption of state resources. Tax expenditures and public aid can be provided both through legislative, regulatory, or administrative tax provisions and through tax administration practices.
A reduction in the tax base or a reduction, total or partial, of the amount of tax, duties or charges, implies a loss of revenue and therefore amounts to the consumption of state resources in the form of tax expenditure.

The notion of aid is broader than that of a subsidy, since it encompasses not only positive measures, such as subsidies themselves, but also state interventions that, in various forms, alleviate the charges that normally weigh on a companys budget and which, therefore, without being subsidies in the strict sense of the word, are of the same nature and have identical effects.
According to a constant jurisprudence, a measure by which public authorities grant to certain companies a tax exemption which, although not involving a transfer of state resources, places the beneficiaries in a more favorable financial situation than other taxpayers constitutes an aid granted by the state or by means of state resources.
The five investment agreements have enabled beneficiaries to be relieved of a portion of the costs they would have normally had to bear themselves in the course of their activity, such as corporate income tax applicable at a given time, normal municipal property tax and the social security contribution generally applicable.
The Authority therefore concludes that the five investment agreements favor certain undertakings and certain productions within the meaning of Article 61, paragraph 1, of the EEA Agreement.

It is settled case law that, for the purpose of qualifying a national measure as state aid, it is not necessary to establish a real impact of the aid on trade between the contracting parties and a genuine distortion of competition, but only to examine whether the aid is likely to affect such trade and distort competition.

In order to apply the clauses relating to state aid provided for in articles 61 to 63 of the EEA Agreement, the state aid must be granted to companies related to the manufacture of products covered by the EEA Agreement.
Article 8, paragraph 3, of the EEA Agreement states the following:
Products included in chapters 25 to 97 of the Harmonized System of Designation and Coding of Goods, except for the products listed in Protocol 2.
The products included in Protocol 3 and subject to the specific arrangements contained in that Protocol.
Article 8, paragraph 3, of the EEA Agreement therefore limits the material scope of application of the EEA Agreement to the products mentioned above, except in cases where the Agreement provides otherwise.
Fish and fishery products, insofar as they do not fall within Chapters 25 to 97 of the Harmonized System of Designation and Coding of Goods or are specified in Protocol 3, are excluded from the scope of application of the EEA Agreement.
Although Article 4 of Protocol 9 of the EEA Agreement states that Aid granted to the fishing sector charged to state resources shall be abolished when it distorts competition, the competence to assess state aid to the fishing sector lies with the contracting parties to the EEA Agreement.
In the opinion of the Organ, the products manufactured by Marmeti fell within chapters 03, 05, 15, 16 and 23 of the Harmonized System of Designation and Coding of Goods (HS) and were therefore outside the scope of the products covered by the EEA Agreement.
Therefore, the Organ is not competent to evaluate the possible aid granted in accordance with the provisions of the Marmeti investment agreement.

The Organ will then assess whether the criteria for the existence of State aid within the meaning of Article 61, paragraph 1, of the EEA Agreement are met for i) the regime in its version amended by the Supplementary Regulation, and ii) the five investment agreements.
For something to be considered state aid, an advantage must be granted by the State or through state funds.
For the application of the rules on state aid, the term State also covers regional and local entities.
A loss of tax revenue is equivalent to the consumption of state funds in the form of tax expenditures, and it can also be granted state aid through tax provisions of a legislative, regulatory or administrative nature, as well as through practices of the tax administration.
A reduction in the tax base or a total or partial reduction in the amount of taxes, fees or charges implies a loss of tax revenue and therefore equates to the consumption of state resources in the form of tax expenditures.

Favor certain companies or productions
In Decision No. 390/10/COL, the Body concluded that the regime was selective, in the sense that only companies investing in certain regions of Iceland and that could benefit from aid under Article 61(3)(c) of the EEA Agreement could receive aid under it.
The Organ accepts the argument of the Icelandic authorities that the modifications to the regime resulting from the Supplementary Regulation expanded the advantages granted to Becromal under the regime.
Therefore, said modifications constitute a selective measure, since they affect a specific company.
The Organ concludes therefore that the Supplementary Regulation favors certain companies and certain productions in the sense of Article 61, paragraph 1, of the EEA Agreement.
In the same way, the beneficiaries of the five investment agreements are individual companies and therefore the measures are selective.
The definition of aid is broader than that of a grant, as it encompasses not only positive benefits, such as grants in their own right, but also state interventions that, in various forms, alleviate the burdens that normally fall on a companys budget and, therefore, while not being grants in the strict sense of the word, have a similar character and produce the same effect.
According to repeated case law, a measure by which public authorities grant certain companies a tax exemption that, although it does not involve a transfer of state funds, places those to whom the exemption is applied in a more favorable financial situation than the rest of the taxpayers, constitutes aid granted by the State or through state resources.
The five investment agreements allowed beneficiaries to be released from part of the costs that they would normally have had to bear in their business activity, such as the payment of corporate tax at the applicable rate at a given time, municipal property tax and general social security contributions.
The Organ concludes, therefore, that the five investment agreements favor certain companies and certain productions in the sense of Article 61, paragraph 1, of the EEA Agreement.
Market distortion and effect on trade between the Contracting Parties
According to settled case law, for the purpose of qualifying a national measure as State aid, it is not necessary to prove the actual impact of the aid on trade between the contracting parties and the actual distortion of competition, but only to examine whether such aid may affect such trade and distort competition.
Furthermore, when aid granted by a Member State of the EEA serves to strengthen the position of a company vis-à-vis other companies that compete with it in intra-EEA exchanges, such exchanges must be considered to be affected by the aid.
The Organ reached the conclusion in Decision 390/10/COL that the regime reinforced the competitive position of the companies benefiting from the aid in the eligible regions, compared to their actual or potential competitors in the EEA.
The Becromal investment agreement strengthens Becromals competitive position compared to other producers of anodized aluminum sheets for electrolytic capacitors.
As Becromal has confirmed, the market for anodized sheets used in aluminum electrolytic capacitors is global, and Becromals clients include companies in [...] and [...].
Therefore, the fact that Becromals main competitors are companies of [...] does not mean that there are no effects on trade in the EEA.
The Verne investment agreement strengthens Vernes competitive position compared to other data centers in Iceland.
Additionally, Verne manages a global wholesale e-commerce data center, whose services will be available to clients in the EEA and the global market.
The Kísilfélagið investment agreement strengthens Kísilfélagiðs competitive position compared to other producers of metallurgical silicon and silica powder.
The market for metallurgical silicon and silica powder is global.
The Thorsil investment agreement strengthens Thorsils competitive position compared to other producers of metallic silicon.
As stated in the preamble of the Thorsil investment agreement, the project was initiated, among other things, to address an increase in demand for metallic silicon from Europe and North America.
The GMR Endurvinnslan investment agreement strengthens GMR Endurvinnslans competitive position compared to other steel recycling companies and steel producers.
The company intends to export most of its products to buyers in the construction sector.
Consequently, the Body concludes that the five investment agreements strengthen the competitive position of the beneficiary companies in the eligible regions compared to their actual or potential competitors in the EEA.

In its Initiation Decision, the Body expressed doubts as to whether the aid granted under the investment agreements had been granted within the scope of the regime, since the agreements appeared, at first glance, to have been affected by the modifications to the regime resulting from the Supplementary Regulation.
In accordance with article 1, letter c), of part II of Protocol 3, modifications to existing aid shall be considered as new aid.
In accordance with Article 4, paragraph 1, of Decision of the Organ No. 195/04/COL, concerning the implementing provisions of Article 27 of Part II of Protocol 3 (hereinafter referred to as the Implementing Decision), [...] any change that does not constitute a modification of a purely formal or administrative nature without repercussions for the assessment of the compatibility of the aid measure with the common market shall be deemed to be a modification of an existing aid.
Therefore, modifications that do not affect the essence of the aid do not affect the way the measure should be classified.
The modifications introduced by the supplementary regulation allow aid to be granted to projects that were started before the grant was awarded, even before the entry into force of the regime.
The modifications introduced by the supplementary regulation alter the basic characteristics of the regime.
In particular, the requirement of the incentive effect regime has been removed.
This requirement was an important part of the assessment of the compatibility of the aid measures granted under the regime, as initially approved (as described in Decision No. 390/10/COL).
Therefore, the Organ considers that the modifications to the regime introduced by the Supplementary Regulation constitute a substantial modification of the Regime as it was approved.
Considering that, by virtue of article 1, letter c), of part II of Protocol 3, modifications of existing aid must be considered new aid, the question arises as to whether the modified existing aid should be considered new aid, or only the modifications as such.

If this were the case, the modification constitutes, as such, a new aid and, therefore, must be assessed in the light of Article 61, paragraph 3, letter c), and of the Guidelines of the Organ on State aid for regional purposes for the period 2007-2013 (hereinafter, the 2007 Guidelines).
According to settled case law, a modification of an existing aid scheme that extends it to a new category of beneficiaries can be clearly distinguished from the initial scheme, since the application of the existing aid scheme to the new category of beneficiaries does not affect the assessment of the compatibility of the initial scheme.
The modified wording of article 3, letter c), of the Regulation on incentives states that the aid may also be granted to a project that began after the conclusion of an investment special agreement and before the entry into force of the supplementary regulation.
This modification expanded the scope of the regime beyond what was approved by Decision of the Organ No. 390/10/COL, by allowing aid to be granted to projects that had begun work before the entry into force of the regime, and which, therefore, would not meet the incentive effect criterion contained in the regime as it was approved.
The supplementary regulation, therefore, opened the regime to a new category of beneficiaries.
On the other hand, the Organ considers that the modifications introduced by the supplementary regulation to articles 8 and 20 of the Regulation on incentives, that is, the reduction of the reference rate for corporate tax and the extension of the maximum duration of tax exemptions, were introduced solely to benefit this new category of beneficiaries.
These new beneficiaries can therefore receive more help than authorized by the initially approved regime.
Therefore, these modifications to the regime are fundamentally interrelated.
However, these modifications do not affect the compatibility of the various measures already covered by the approved regime.
Therefore, the Organ considers that the modifications introduced by the Supplementary Regulation constitute an alteration that can be clearly dissociated from the initial regime.
Therefore, the Body considers these modifications to the regime to be a new aid measure.
Likewise, it can be deduced that the aid granted on the basis of the Supplementary Regulation will also be considered new aid.

In the interest of exhaustiveness and in order to respond to the allegations made by Becromal and the Icelandic authorities, the Body has examined the Becromal investment agreement subsidiarily as an ad hoc measure.
In this regard, the Body refers to the DAR of 2007 and, in particular, to the incentive effect requirement that appears in them.
In accordance with section 30 of the DAR of 2007, the existence of an incentive effect is a necessary prerequisite for compatibility:
The objective of section 30 of the 2007 Guidelines for State Aid is to allow the Authority to determine whether investment aid has a sufficiently stimulating effect, without carrying out a full analysis of the economic circumstances of the beneficiarys investment decision at the time it was made.

Therefore, as confirmed by the General Court in the Kronoply judgment, the Body may base its assessment of the incentive effect on a chronological circumstance.
Becromal has argued that the assessment of state aid matters has changed with the introduction of a more detailed economic analysis, so that when evaluating the incentive effect of the aid, the Body must also assess its economic nature (that is, whether it has contributed to a change in the beneficiarys behavior), taking into account the provisions of the Guidelines on regional aid for large investment projects.
The Organ cannot accept this argument.
Becromal refers to the judgment of the Court of Justice in the HGA case to argue that: «[…] the Court, in its examination of the judgment of the First Instance Court, states that the only factor determining whether the aid measure had an incentive effect is examining whether it changed the investors behavior, and it did not take into account all the references that the First Instance Court had made to the Kronoply case».
However, Becromals interpretation of the HGA judgment does not take into account the express statement by the Court of Justice that the General Courts finding that «[…] the priority of the request for aid over the start of the application of the investment project is a simple, relevant and appropriate criterion that allows the Commission to presume the necessary character of the projected aid» was not challenged in the appeal procedure.
Therefore, the Body opines that in this case it is necessary to base its conclusion of incompatibility on a chronological evaluation: namely, that i) the incentive requirement established in the 2007 DARs was not met, and ii) work on the project began before the investment agreement was signed and the grant was awarded.
The Organ also rejects Becromals claim that the Organ must examine the project in light of the Guidelines on Regional Aid for Large Investment Projects, and apply the requirement of the incentive effect established in them.
The Organ notes that, for some large investment projects, the test of the incentive effect established in point 30 of the 2007 DAR (based on the chronological requirement that an application has been submitted and eligibility confirmed before work begins) is supplemented by a second, economic test (based on an economic analysis that the aid contributes to a change in the beneficiarys behavior), provided for in section 57 of the Guidelines on regional aid and in the Guidelines on regional aid for large investment projects.
The Guidelines on regional aid for large investment projects provide for an in-depth assessment of certain large investment projects, namely those that have significant effects on trade and may lead to substantial distortions of competition.
According to section 3 of the Guidelines on regional aid for large investment projects and section 49 of the 2007 State Aid Regulations, a large investment project is understood to be an initial investment whose eligible expenses exceed 50 million EUR.
As previously mentioned, the Becromal investment agreement refers to 117.25 million EUR as the total cost of the investment for the two phases of the project.
However, neither Becromal nor the Icelandic authorities have submitted any information to the Body regarding the intensity of the aid or the eligible expenses of the project.
They have also not provided information on the conditions for the detailed verification obligation established in the Guidelines on Regional Aid for Large Investment Projects referred to in point 57, letters a) and b), of the 2007 State Aid Regulations (i.e., that the beneficiary of the aid has more than 25% of the products in question on the reference market, and that the production capacity created by the project represents more than 5% of the reference product market).
In the opinion of the Organ, nothing indicates that the Becromal project exceeds the maximum limits foreseen in section 57 of the 2007 Guidelines.
The Organ concludes, therefore, that the in-depth analysis provided for in the Guidelines on regional aid for large investment projects does not apply to the Becromal investment agreement.
Consequently, in light of the case law and the wording of the 2007 DARs, it is sufficient to check whether the request for assistance has been submitted by the beneficiary and whether the granting authority has provided a written confirmation, conditional upon the approval of the measure by the Body, before the commencement of work on the project.
The 2007 DARs define the term start of work as the start of construction work, or the first firm commitment for the order of equipment, excluding prior feasibility studies.
As confirmed by Icelandic authorities and Becromal, construction work began on the Becromal project in 2008, even before the signing of the previous agreement on July 7, 2009.
The Organ cannot accept Becromals thesis that the start of work should be limited to orders for equipment designed specifically for the project in question, and that the acquisition of land or the construction of a plant does not necessarily mark the beginning of an investment decision, since such an investment could be easily sold at some point.
This interpretation is corroborated by the jurisprudence of the General Court, according to which even a reduced level of investment by the beneficiary is sufficient to produce legal effects, and it is therefore considered to constitute the first phase of the work.
On the other hand, the information available to the Body shows that Becromal began construction of the plant in early 2008, and its procurement process began at the end of that year.
